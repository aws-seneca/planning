# AWS x Seneca — Master Plan

---

## Vision

AWS x Seneca is not just another cloud club. It is a club for engineers to talk about everything that touches cloud — DevOps, system design, job prep, AWS and Amazon connections, and more. The goal is to give back what so many have given before: real knowledge, real thinking, real community.

In two years, students should leave Seneca with systems-thinking baked in, skills that go well beyond what Seneca's curriculum alone provides, and connections that follow them into their careers. The club should continue after its founders leave. That continuity is part of the mission.

AWS x Seneca only wants people dedicated to the vision — people who put extra effort into teaching others, who make one more person show up, who make one more person excited about this. The ideal culture is one where members contribute outside of meetings, share ideas without hesitation, and are genuinely excited by the work. That energy should not be limited to executives.

---

## Culture

The core mission is simple: A community of student builders, empowering them to learn, build and connect with everything related to Cloud and AWS.

- Contribution over attendance. Showing up matters less than giving something.
- No gatekeeping. If you are curious, you belong here.
- Ideas from anyone. Excitement is the only credential required to pitch something.
- Engineers teaching engineers. The best sessions come from people who just figured something out and want to share it.
- Build in public. Projects, learning, and progress should be visible to the group.

---

## Curriculum

The curriculum covers everything that connects to cloud — not just AWS services in isolation, but the engineering context around them.

### Core topics

1. **Foundations of core AWS services** — EC2, S3, IAM, RDS, serverless (Lambda, API Gateway, DynamoDB), VPC, security groups
2. **System design and how it fits with AWS** — How to think in systems, then how AWS maps to each layer including ALB, Route 53, CloudFront, ElastiCache, and patterns for scale
3. **DevOps and deploying to cloud** — CI/CD pipelines, Docker, ECR, ECS, GitHub Actions, CloudWatch, X-Ray, and infrastructure as code
4. **Cost optimization** — How AWS billing works, reserved vs spot vs on-demand, when serverless saves money and when it does not

## 8-Event Annual Curriculum

Additional monthly online community events like: 

Service deep dive — pick one AWS service, go from zero to something deployed in 45 minutes. Lambda, S3 static hosting, RDS setup — contained enough to finish in one session.

"How does X actually work" — not a tutorial, more of a conceptual walkthrough. How does CloudFront actually cache things? How does IAM actually evaluate permissions? These work great online because it's just a conversation with a diagram.

Member project showcase — someone shares something they built, walks through the architecture, takes questions. Zero prep needed from the host, builds the "build in public" culture.

## Year One Goals

- **75+ members** who are genuinely engaged, not just signed up
- Members who get more excited about future events as the year goes on — momentum compounds
- At least one person who comes from outside Seneca, drawn purely by the quality of the events
- A culture where people contribute between meetings, not just at them
- A leadership pipeline so the club survives after its founders leave

---

## Notes

- Certifications are a running thread across the year, not a standalone event. Introduce the Cloud Practitioner path at AWS 101, revisit at the career panel, track progress as a group goal. Check whether SBG leader perks include exam vouchers.
- GDG × SBG cross-pollination happens through how events are run and promoted, not as a one-off collab event. Shared audience, shared network.
- Real projects and real infrastructure should anchor technical sessions wherever possible. Showing a deployed production app changes how people receive the content.
- AWS credits should be prioritized for the hackathon. If available, they lower the barrier to building and make the flagship event more ambitious.
